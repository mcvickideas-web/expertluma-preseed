Why they stall02

A good demo is not a sign-off.

01

The score hides the miss

Most cases look fine, so the slide says ship it. The case that would hurt the business is lost in the average. Someone has to check those cases. Someone else has to accept the check.

02

A new version borrows an old yes

The model changes. The prompt changes. The name stays the same. Last quarter’s test is used to justify this quarter’s system. Nobody has shown that this version is actually better.

03

Nobody owns the yes

The score says 98%. The policy says compliant. The vendor says ready. A person at the company still has to put their name on it. Today that yes lives in a slide, a spreadsheet, and a meeting.

They do not need another model. They need a way to decide whether this one is allowed to do this job.

How it works04

Three questions. Then they sign, or they don’t.

01

Were the hard cases checked?

People do the work. A second person accepts it. Handing work in is not the same as approving it. The customer trains the model. We do not.

02

Did this version hold up?

We test this exact version on the cases that must not fail. A high score does not erase a miss that matters. A new name is not a new system.

03

Will they sign?

Yes, send it back, or not yet. If the proof is not there, the answer stays not yet. We will not paint that green to raise money.

They make the call. We do not sign for them. The product is willing to say not yet, and that is the point.
