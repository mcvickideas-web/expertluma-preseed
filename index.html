The stop

A high score is how the AI gets shipped.

Companies already have a model, an agent, or a vendor product. The average looks fine, so the slide says go live. The miss that actually matters never owns the decision.

01 · The work

Judgment

Someone has to review the cases that must not fail. Today that sits in a labeling tool, a spreadsheet, and an inbox.

We produce the judgment and accept it. Submitting is not accepting.

02 · The test

This version

Scores average the miss away. Most cases passed, so a required failure never stops the program. A rename is treated as improvement.

We measure this version for this use. A required miss can block while the score stays high.

03 · The permission

A name

The evidence, the evaluation, and the approval live apart. “Looks good enough” has no owner.

A named person at their company decides. Use it, send it back, or not ready. We do not grant the permission.

Where the company is

You can walk the plant. You cannot call it traction.

You can see this

  • A program produces work against a defined job.
  • A person submits. Inspection and acceptance are separate.
  • This version can be measured. Evidence is kept.
  • A required failure can block the outcome.
  • The same version is refused as an improvement.
  • Money follows accepted work. No agreed price means nothing is owed.

You cannot treat this as traction

  • Zero customers. Zero revenue.
  • The demonstrations are ours. No uncoached stranger has paid.
  • No SOC 2. No live patient data.
  • A buyer cannot yet log into a hosted room without us.
  • A UK company is registered. A bank account is not open. Stripe Atlas is the plan.
The technical risk is largely taken. The risk in this round is commercial: will a company put a real AI through the system and pay for the evidence and the decision?
