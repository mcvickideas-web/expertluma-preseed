The problem02

Someone has to own the failure.

A company built an AI to handle payments: pay it, hold it, or send it to a person. The demo says 98%. The one payment that should have been held is inside that 98%.

What they have

Three answers

A labeling vendor’s spreadsheet. Last quarter’s accuracy slide. A meeting where someone is asked to say yes.

What they lack

One production record

This version. The cases that matter. Who reviewed them. What changed. What evidence supports the decision.

Who feels it

The executive

The person who eventually has to answer: why did we allow this AI to do this job?

The problem is not that the AI has no score. The problem is that the company cannot defend the decision behind the score.

What they buy03

One AI. One job. One answer.

The first check

$25–75KDeposit, then the rest when the answer is delivered

They bring the AI they already have. We do not build another one, and we do not train it.

What the money buys

  • Produce. Required training and evaluation material is created, inspected, and accepted.
  • Validate. This exact AI version is measured against a defined examination.
  • Remediate. Important failures return to production. They do not disappear into an average.
  • Certify. Evidence is assembled against the requirements for the intended job.
  • Decide. The customer makes the authorization decision. The system preserves what that decision relied on.
They bring the AI. We run the production around it. We have not sold this yet.
