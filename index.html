The wedge02

Enterprise AI has three problems before it can be authorized.

01

The difficult cases

The average score looks good. The failure that matters is buried in the average. Someone still has to produce, inspect, and accept the evidence behind the cases that must not fail.

02

This version

A model changes. A prompt changes. A tool changes. The name stays the same. Yesterday’s result is used to justify today’s system. Nobody has shown that this exact version improved.

03

The authorization

Evaluation says 98%. Governance says compliant. The vendor says production-ready. Someone still has to answer: are we willing to authorize this AI for this job?

Companies already have the model. They do not need another one. The gap is between “the AI works” and “we will authorize it.”

How it works04

Three questions. One production system.

Train

Was the material produced?

Did we produce the evidence and the work this job requires? The customer trains the model in their environment. We do not.

Produce → Inspect → Accept → Release

Validate

Did this version hold?

Did this exact AI demonstrate the required behavior, including the cases that must not fail? A higher score does not clear a required miss. A rename is not an improvement.

Examine → Measure → Critical cases → Evidence

Certify

Is it enough to authorize?

Is the evidence sufficient to authorize this AI for this job, under these conditions? If it is not, the system says not ready.

Evidence → Conditions → Decision

The customer makes the authorization decision. We do not certify an AI on our own authority. Painting a refusal green to raise money would be the failure.
